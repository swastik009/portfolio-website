Blackout sweeps the nation

updated 11:19 PM PT

Most of the continental United States is without power tonight. Grid operators report a cascade of failures that began near Boston at about 2:04 a.m. Eastern time and crossed the country in less than ten minutes. Parts of Canada and northern Mexico are also dark.

Officials were quick to rule out the Year 2000 bug. "The East Coast passed midnight two hours earlier without a single fault," said a spokesman for the Department of Energy. "The West Coast hasn't reached midnight yet. This is not Y2K." He would not say what it is.

What we know

  • The FAA has grounded all flights. Airports are on emergency power.
  • Hospitals, emergency services and some radio and TV stations are running on generators.
  • A few scattered neighborhoods came back within minutes. Nobody can explain the pattern.
  • In one of those neighborhoods the automated monorail started running again, on schedule, with nobody aboard. Transit officials say they did not restart it.
  • The telephone network is largely working. Phone switches have their own batteries; carriers ask customers to keep lines free for emergencies.
  • The President will address the nation by radio.

Cascadia Bell: lines holding

The Northwest's phone company says its 214 switching offices are on battery and generator power and "operating normally." Asked whether its network could have played a part in the outage, a spokeswoman said, "No," and hung up.
